After some disturbing online commentary in response to our non-partisan initiative connecting early-career researchers with local policymakers, we wanted to clarify our objectives as the Scientist Network for Advancing Policy (SNAP). Our coalition serves to advocate for policies that improve the American scientific enterprise, as well as support the early career researchers who so often perform the bulk of the scientific work that powers innovation.

We thank the 65 members of Congress from both sides of the aisle who have hosted productive and meaningful conversations with SNAP members about the future of American science and STEM workforce development. We were deeply disappointed to discover vitriolic comments from social media users, including racist remarks, directed toward one trainee following their meeting.

SNAP was founded on the principle that science does not exist in a silo. Science is a reflection of the human beings behind it: diverse and complex by nature. When our humanity remains hidden, some may forget it exists. Scientists, just like everyone else, deserve dignity, respect, and empowerment to advocate for meaningful change. Humanizing science, to communicate not only our work as scientists but also who we are and what drives us, is urgently needed and lies at the core of SNAP’s mission. The public deserves to know where their tax dollars go, and scientists, as fellow community members, deserve to share how our work advances the common good. Respect is essential for us all.
